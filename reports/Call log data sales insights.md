# Sequence every number to turn call logs into KPIs

Callyzer can deliver most of the "senior data analyst in a box" without new hardware or recordings. The fields it already collects become real sales KPIs once the product stops counting calls per rep and starts **ordering every call to and from each customer number across all reps in an account**. That sequence produces metrics no per-rep Excel export can reproduce without heavy work: missed-call callback time and never-returned rate, attempts to first connect, give-up depth, first response on new numbers, follow-up gaps, leads going cold, duplicate calling between reps, and over-dialling risk. Connect rate is a weak KPI. Vendors define it inconsistently, it counts IVR pickups and two-second hang-ups, and a single national figure averages away the industry differences that matter ([Orum](https://www.orum.com/blog/how-to-improve-sales-connect-rates-in-2026); [TitanX](https://titanx.beehiiv.com/p/fake-connect-rates-are-a-real-problem)). The research also shows that delivery matters as much as the metrics: only about **25–30% of employees use the BI dashboards their companies pay for** ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/3753469); [G2](https://www.g2.com/articles/business-intelligence-statistics)). The large customer's habit of exporting the data to Excel is the expected outcome of a pull dashboard. The answer is a pushed, ranked, small daily brief on WhatsApp and email, backed by a dashboard for drill-down. For marketing, the 242M-call Sep–Oct 2026 dataset can support India's first public benchmarks on missed-call callback speed, attempts-to-connect and conversation depth. None of these exists for India today, and the US studies most vendors quote are 2007–2013 vintage and widely misquoted. Two caveats run through every recommendation. Outcome KPIs (site visits, disbursals, promises to pay) need one or two extra fields, mainly a one-tap disposition and a lead status. Several regulatory points (RBI recovery-call rules effective 2027, TRAI's 10-digit telemarketing restriction, IRDAI recording disclosure, DPDP Rules phasing in now) need legal review before they are built into product or marketing claims.

## Connect rate fails as a KPI because the value sits in the sequence

A connect rate tells a sales head that 46% of dials were answered. It does not say which leads were missed, which reps gave up after one try, or which customers called back and never heard from anyone. Practitioners define "connect" inconsistently. Some vendors count voicemail and IVR pickups, and others require a real conversation. JustCall's "contact rate" excludes quick hang-ups and wrong numbers, and a common practitioner cutoff for a "conversation" is **≥30 seconds of talk** ([Prospeo](https://prospeo.io/s/connect-rate); [JustCall](https://justcall.io/blog/contact-rate-vs-connection-rate-vs-answer-rate.html)). Callyzer's own published figure of a **46–48% average connect rate, peaking at 61.79% at 5 PM**, sits far above the ~5% per-dial connect rates quoted for US cold calling ([Callyzer](https://callyzer.co/blog/the-ultimate-list-of-cold-calling-statistics-in-india/); [JustCall](https://justcall.io/blog/auto-dialer-benchmarks-2026.html)). That gap is mostly definitional, because it covers any answered call from a known 10-digit SIM, including warm follow-ups. It is not evidence that calling works better in India. The user is right that a connect-rate benchmark on its own is not a KPI.

Contact-centre KPIs give the right vocabulary, but SIM teams have no ACD queue, so each one needs a translation. Average speed of answer becomes **missed-call callback time**. Abandonment becomes the **unreturned missed-call rate**. Occupancy becomes **talk time as a share of shift**. First-contact resolution becomes a **single-touch proxy**, which stays ambiguous until a disposition exists ([LeadDesk](https://leaddesk.com/blog/complete-guide-to-call-center-metrics/); [SQM](https://sqmgroup.com/resources/library/blog/top-10-customer-service-metrics)). Right-party contact, which collections teams manage to, cannot be derived from metadata at all ([Five9](https://www.five9.com/faq/what-is-right-party-contacts-rpc)). This translation work is itself the product: Callyzer must publish fixed, org-configurable definitions so customers stop rebuilding them in Excel.

The building blocks are as follows. All are design choices from the research synthesis, not industry standards:

| Building block | Definition (default; org-configurable) |
|---|---|
| Org timeline T(N) | All calls to/from normalised number N across every rep in the account, sorted by timestamp. Internal numbers (employee SIMs, office lines) and a personal-contact blocklist are excluded |
| Connected call | duration > 0. Publish sensitivity at 0 s, 10 s and 30 s |
| Conversation | duration ≥ T_conv. Default 60 s for B2C telecalling and 120 s for qualification-heavy sales (loans, real estate) |
| Duration buckets | 0 s not connected · 1–9 s hang-up · 10–29 s brief · 30–119 s short · 120–299 s qualifying · ≥300 s deep |
| New number | First event falls in the window, with no event in the previous L days (look-back washout: L=90 for transactional sales, 180 for real estate) |
| Missed episode | A run of missed/rejected inbound calls from N with no connected call in between. Count episodes, not raw missed calls, because customers retry two or three times |
| Episode / cold gap | G = 14 idle days separates activity bursts on a number |
| Business-hours clock | Response times are reported both as clock time and as in-shift time, so night leads do not distort medians |

## A prioritized catalogue of 25 KPIs from five fields

Difficulty runs from *Simple* (an aggregate over rep and day) through *Sequence* (per-number ordering across reps) to *Statistical* (baselines, confidence intervals, models). The formulas are synthesised designs. Where an external benchmark anchors a default threshold, the "Why it matters" column cites it.

## Nine Indian verticals share an activity layer and differ on one conversion event

Across verticals, managers review the same activity layer every day: dials, connects, talk time, follow-ups due, speed to first call, and missed inbound calls. Each vertical then adds one physical-world conversion event that defines success. The split that matters for product design is inbound-heavy versus outbound-heavy. Inbound-heavy teams (healthcare, logistics via IndiaMART, auto enquiries, portal-fed real estate) live or die on callback speed. Outbound-heavy teams (EdTech, loans, collections, solar) live on attempts, conversation rate and talk time. Published Indian norms are thin and come almost entirely from **job descriptions and vendor blogs**. They cluster at **80–200 dials/day and 2–4 hours (up to 5) of talk time** for counsellor and telesales roles ([TeamLease JD](https://www.teamlease.com/jobs/academic-counsellors-jobs-in-delhi-gurgaon-veranda-race-670458); [CollegeDunia JD](https://jobs.weekday.works/career-counsellor-at-collegedunia-4419805132); [upGrad Abroad JD](https://career.io/job/senior-admissions-counselor-pune-upgrad-abroad-a247da56388a1c11a165278799b97b67)). Callyzer's own blog cites ~80 calls/day as a floor and 68 calls/day in Indore vs 92 in Mumbai ([Callyzer KPIs](https://callyzer.co/blog/telesales-kpis-every-telecalling-team-must-track/); [Callyzer WFH benchmarks](https://callyzer.co/blog/wfh-telesales-performance-india/)). Two inferences from the vertical work shape the product. First, an industry template should switch the **default threshold set** (T_conv, follow-up SLA, cold gap G, look-back L), not just the dashboard layout. A 60-second conversation is meaningful in healthcare booking and meaningless in a real-estate qualification. Second, several conversion events can be proxied from timing patterns, such as a short outgoing call within 24 hours of a known visit date. **No source has validated how accurately these proxies predict site visits, test drives or PTPs**, so they should ship labelled "proxy" until a customer-data study calibrates them.

## Competitors sell transcript AI; metadata-native insight is open ground

The global market splits into two layers. Metadata dashboards (volume, connect rate, talk time, unanswered calls, leaderboards) come bundled or as modest add-ons, such as Aircall's Analytics+ at a cited ~$15/licence/month, which also lifts a 6-month history cap ([Quo](https://www.quo.com/blog/aircall-pricing/); [Rezora](https://rezora.io/blog/aircall-pricing)). Almost every marketed "AI" feature depends on recordings and transcripts: Dialpad's AI Scorecards, available only on Advanced/Premium plans ([Dialpad Help](https://help.dialpad.com/docs/AI-scorecard-analytics)), RingCentral's RingSense at a cited ~$60/user ([Retell AI](https://www.retellai.com/blog/ringcentral-pricing)), and Gong's deal warnings and Ask Anything ([Gong Help](https://help.gong.io/understanding-ai-deal-monitor)). Metadata-derived recommendations exist only in narrow forms inside dialers. Orum has "Best Time to Call" ([Orum](https://www.orum.com/platform/analytics)). Kixie shows per-list connect rates on a dashboard it brands "AI" even though it is mostly metadata ([Kixie](https://support.kixie.com/hc/en-us/articles/55213444516507-Kixie-AI-Dashboard-Introduction)). HubSpot still cannot report more finely than daily ([HubSpot Community](https://community.hubspot.com/t5/Dashboards-Reporting/hourly-call-reporting/m-p/538418)).

In India, missed-call capture and follow-up reminders are table stakes. The research found **no evidence that any SIM-based Indian CRM packages the following as automated insight**: missed-call recovery measured as an SLA (median callback time, % within X minutes), speed-to-lead on new numbers, attempts-to-connect and give-up depth, cross-rep collision detection, segment-level best-time-to-call, gaming detection, data-trust indicators, scheduled WhatsApp or email digests, or LLM-written narratives over metadata. That is the whitespace. One caveat matters: callyzer.co, telecrm.in and several competitor sites could not be fetched, so **this absence is a hypothesis to confirm through competitor demos**, not a proven fact. Callyzer's own current report list was also not confirmed.

Customer complaints point to the same opportunity. Gong reviewers call the product "overwhelming" and hard to customise to their own KPIs ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/gong/reviews?page=3)). TeleCRM users ask for longer-period charts ([G2](https://www.g2.com/products/telecrm/reviews)). Buyers do not want more reports. They want fewer insights they can trust, and Callyzer's position (SIM-native, no recording dependence, low price) suits a **metadata-native insight layer**. That layer also avoids the consent burden and cost of transcripts.

## The evidence supports speed and persistence but not the famous multipliers

Most sales-calling "stats" in circulation trace back to a handful of US studies from 2007–2013, and the popular versions overstate them. Callyzer's marketing should quote the real findings precisely and present its own India data as the corrective. The grading below uses the evidence notes' labels: primary study, vendor dataset, or myth.

|---|---|---|---|

Publish absolute contact rates by callback-time bucket from Indian data |
| "Harvard says respond in an hour" | HBR 2011 was a secret-shopper audit of 2,241 US firms. 37% replied within an hour, 23% never replied, and the average was ~42 h ([BYU ScholarsArchive](https://scholarsarchive.byu.edu/facpub/9711); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/147757/customer-leads-cant-wait-forever.html)). It measured slowness, not conversion. Sub-analysis figures circulating online were not verified | Primary, US | Mirror its format: "X% of missed calls in India are never returned" |
| "Only 7% respond within 5 minutes" | Drift 2017, 433 mostly SaaS firms. The "47-hour average" attached to it is disputed ([Salesloft/Drift](https://salesloft.com/resources/blog/lead-response-survey)) | Vendor secret-shopper | Cite only the 7% |

These measure reaching the lead, not selling | Vendor, large data | Test both on Indian data. A 242M-call dataset dwarfs Velocify's |
| "80% of sales need 5 follow-ups; 44% give up after one" | Traced to a 1942 survey of fewer than 40 people and an uncited 2009 column. A "single-attempt abandonment rate" from real data replaces it |
| "Best time to call is 4–5 PM Thursday" | The InsideSales data says Thu/Wed and 4–5 PM ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/best-practices-responding-to-online-leads)). WAVV's 85M dials favour Monday ([WAVV](https://www.wavv.com/post/best-time-to-call)). Cognism says Tuesday 10–11 AM. In India, Callyzer says 3–5 PM is best and 11–12 worst, while Caller.Digital says 11:00–13:00 is better ([Callyzer](https://callyzer.co/blog/best-time-to-call-customers-india/); [Caller.Digital](https://caller.digital/blog/connect-rate-benchmark-outbound-calling-india-2026)) | Conflicting vendor data | Publish by industry with method and CIs. A single national hour is likely to suffer from Simpson's paradox |
| "Calls over 60/90 s are qualified" | A practitioner convention. **No research validates it against outcomes** ([Unbounce community](https://community.unbounce.com/industry-best-practices-strategy-36/let-s-talk-callrail-1133)). Gong's duration evidence is mixed | Convention | Use tiered buckets and calibrate per customer |
| "43:57 talk-to-listen ratio" | 25,537 B2B SaaS discovery calls averaging 43 minutes ([HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/sales/best-sales-calls-25537-calls)). Says nothing about short Indian telecalls | Vendor, context-specific | Not applicable to metadata |
| "62% of small-business calls go unanswered" | Based on an 85-business study from 2016 ([SalesAI](https://salesai.com/blog/missed-call-statistics)) | Weak | Replace it with Indian missed-call data |
| "60–70% of dashboards go unused (Gartner)" | No Gartner source could be found. The traceable figure is ~25–30% BI adoption ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/3753469)) | **Untraceable** | Do not cite it in marketing |

India context runs through all of this. Truecaller counted **~4,168 crore spam calls in India in 2025, with 36% from sales/telemarketing** ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/indians-received-over-4000-crore-spam-calls-in-2025-1189-crore-blocked-report-515765-2026-02-11)). A Kantar survey for Truecaller and Tata Tele found that **79% of businesses say customers avoid unknown numbers while 76% of consumers say they prefer calls**, and that 49% would engage more if they could schedule callbacks ([The Wire/PTI](https://m.thewire.in/article/ptiprnews/79-percent-indians-avoid-unknown-business-calls-reveals-truecaller-and-tata-tele-business-services-report)). Answer rates are therefore under structural pressure. That makes first-call vs repeat-call answer rates, and customer callbacks, more important to measure in India than in the US data the classic studies describe. No India-specific study exists for speed-to-lead, attempt curves, duration–conversion links or callback latency. Callyzer's dataset can fill that gap, provided results are framed as correlational and labelled "among teams using Callyzer".

## Push a three-item brief, not a faster dashboard

Fixing dashboard lag is necessary, but the evidence says it will not create an analyst. Self-service BI reaches roughly a quarter to a third of potential users, and that share has barely moved in years ([G2](https://www.g2.com/articles/business-intelligence-statistics)). About half of senior decision-makers feel overwhelmed by the volume of dashboards they receive ([DigitalisationWorld](https://digitalisationworld.com/news/69088/half-of-executives-feel-overwhelmed-by-the-sheer-volume-of-data-and-dashboards-they-receive-daily)). Gartner forecast that automatically generated "data stories" would become the dominant way people consume analytics, though no source checks whether that happened ([TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchbusinessanalytics/feature/Gartner-predicts-data-storytelling-will-dominate-BI-by-2025)). Push has its own failure mode. In clinical systems, **72.5% of alerts were dismissed in under 3 seconds**, and responsiveness falls as cumulative alert exposure rises ([JMIR 2022](https://www.jmir.org/2022/2/e23355)). The design lever is therefore the *number* of pushes per manager per week, gated statistically the way Amplitude (outside a 99% confidence interval) and Mixpanel (which warns about hourly false positives) do ([Amplitude](https://www.amplitude.com/docs/analytics/anomaly-forecast); [Mixpanel](https://docs.mixpanel.com/docs/features/alerts)). Even Gong customers build digests by hand, which suggests the format fills a real gap ([Gong Visioneers](https://visioneers.gong.io/internal-collaboration-75/anyone-sending-gong-digests-to-your-sales-team-353)).

The recommended package treats the dashboard as the system of record and adds a small, ranked "system of engagement" on top:

| Module | What it is | Content & rules | Evidence / precedent | Tier suggestion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Morning brief (WhatsApp + email)** | Three ranked items with a deep link, at a fixed time daily | Example: "14 missed calls from new numbers not returned (list)"; "Rahul: conversations −40% vs his 4-week average; 70% of calls under 10 s"; "23 active leads going cold >7 days". Each item states what changed, the evidence, so-what, suggested action, link and sample size | Fixed cadence and sections, as in Zoho Books' weekly email ([Zoho](https://www.zoho.com/in/books/kb/reports/weekly-summary-report.md)). Demand evident from third-party WhatsApp report bots ([WA.Expert](https://wa.expert/pages/whatsapp-sales-report-bot)). No Indian SaaS was found sending an analytics digest on WhatsApp, so this would be a differentiator | Insights tier |
| **Weekly digest (email/PDF)** | Team trends, rep scorecard summary, cohort view, best-time heatmap | 5 sections maximum. Week-over-week change with CIs | Zoho pattern; Gong community practice | Insights tier |
| **Real-time alerts** | Only rare, high-severity events | Zero calls by 11 AM on a workday; a new-number missed call unreturned after 2 h; RBI-window breach; device silent >X h in shift | Alert-fatigue evidence argues for a hard cap | Base (few) / Insights (configurable) |
| **Rep-private scorecard** | Each rep sees their own composite vs their own baseline | Conversations to unique leads, follow-up compliance, callback speed. **No public ranking on raw volume or duration** | Removing rank feedback raised performance ~11% in one experiment ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332742029_How_Do_One's_Peers_on_a_Leaderboard_Affect_Oneself)). Private feedback gave +3.8% in a retail field study ([Univ. Bern](https://www.iop.unibe.ch/unibe/portal/fak_wiso/a_bwl/inst_op/content/e39703/e49071/e1648370/e49072/e49073/pane373902/e705318/Real-TimeFeedbackandSales_duringshift_final_ger.pdf)). 56% of monitored workers feel stressed vs 40% of others ([APA 2023](https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/work-in-america/2023-work-america-ai-monitoring)). Single-metric targets invite gaming ([CX Today](https://cxtoday.com/your-wem-strategy-isnt-improving-engagement-its-teaching-agents-how-to-game-the-system)) | Base |
| **"Ask Callyzer" (AI Q&A)** | Natural-language questions in English/Hinglish, scoped to account, team, rep and period | The LLM extracts structured parameters (metric, dimension, filter, period) from ~20–40 canonical metrics and does not write free SQL. Each answer shows the definition, window and row count. Seed ~50 verified answers. Ask for clarification when a question is ambiguous | Text-to-SQL scored only 21% on the original Spider 2.0 enterprise benchmark vs ~73% on BIRD ([Agami](https://blog.agami.ai/text-to-sql-accuracy-on-real-company-data/)). Accuracy recovers with a semantic layer and verified answers ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/power-bi/create-reports/copilot-prepare-data-ai-verified-answers)) | Insights / premium |
| **Data-trust panel** | Sync freshness, silent devices, duplicate rows, unmapped call types | Shown on every report header | Answers accuracy complaints | Base |

Three design rules matter most. **The LLM narrates; code computes.** Every number in a digest comes from deterministic metric code, which removes most hallucination risk. **Small teams need small-sample discipline.** Require at least ~30 calls before flagging, compare each rep with their own rolling baseline using medians and MAD, require persistence over two or more days, suppress repeat alerts, and cap each digest at 3–5 items ranked by estimated impact. **Let managers tune the feed.** Mute buttons and "useful / not useful" feedback are how Gong and Mixpanel keep alerts relevant, and they give Callyzer adoption telemetry. The Insights tier itself copies a common global upsell pattern: longer history, alerts, digests, SLA tracking and AI on top of base reports. Basic reports should stay in the base plan for price-sensitive Indian SMBs. There is **no independent evidence on digest open rates or optimal cadence**, so the large customer should be run as a design partner with measured weekly active usage.

## Fifteen public benchmarks the 242M-call dataset can own

Callyzer's existing connect-rate tool and its "5 crore telecalls" stats are a start. The Sep–Oct 2026 dataset (242M calls, 24 industries) is about five times larger and can anchor an annual **"State of Telecalling in India"** report. The formats that earned Indian mainstream pickup share a crore-scale denominator, one quotable counter-intuitive number, category splits and an annual cadence, as Truecaller's reports did across Business Today, ThePrint/ANI and India TV ([ThePrint/ANI](https://theprint.in/ani-press-releases/indias-spam-shield-the-truecaller-community-blocked-nearly-1200-crore-unwanted-calls-in-2025/2851847)). Gong's "300M calls" Labs series earns repeated citations from stat-roundup sites ([Gong Labs](https://www.gong.io/resources/labs/the-best-and-worst-cold-call-openers-backed-by-data-from-300m-calls/)). Pairing each metric with the US myth it tests adds a link-earning angle.

| Priority | Benchmark metric (by industry) | Classic claim it tests | Likely headline format |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Missed-call never-returned rate** (no outbound within 24 h / 7 days) | HBR "23% never respond" | "X% of missed calls to Indian sales teams are never returned" |
| 2 | **Missed-call callback latency** (median, P75) by industry and hour | Speed-to-lead | "Indian [industry] teams take a median N min to call back a missed call" |

The methodology page is what makes these citable, and it guards against embarrassment if the new data contradicts Callyzer's earlier 61.79%-at-5 PM claim. It should commit to the following. Publish definitions and sensitivity at 0/10/30 s, because "connect" varies so widely ([Orum](https://www.orum.com/blog/how-to-improve-sales-connect-rates-in-2026)). Show account-weighted medians alongside call-weighted ones, so a few high-volume collection accounts do not dominate. Report n (accounts, reps, calls) per industry and suppress cells with fewer than ~30 accounts, since industry is self-reported. Use medians and percentiles for heavy-tailed durations and latencies, capping outliers. Normalise +91/0 prefixes and exclude internal, toll-free, short-code, OTP/IVR and 140/1600-series numbers. Handle right-censoring by fixing a follow-up horizon, for example counting only numbers first seen before 24 October for a 7-day window. Flag Navratri, Dussehra and pre-Diwali days and do not generalise the figures to a full year. Attribute callbacks at account level, so a colleague's return call is not counted as "never returned". State that timing results are correlational. Publish only aggregates with minimum cell sizes. Exact field semantics need confirming before metrics 1–3 and 10 are computed: whether "rejected", "blocked" and "never attended" are distinct call types, and whether numbers are mapped across reps at account level.

## Five extra fields unlock the outcome layer

Metadata gets Callyzer to activity, responsiveness, persistence and depth. Outcomes need very little more, provided each field costs a rep under two seconds. Disposition adoption must itself be measured (untagged share of conversations ≥T_conv), because no Indian benchmark for it exists.

| Rank | Field | Capture method | KPIs unlocked |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One-tap post-call disposition** (shown when dur>0): Interested · Call back later [time] · Not interested · Wrong person/number · Already purchased · Converted | A pop-up after each connected call | Right-party contact, interest rate, callback-promise compliance, true first-contact resolution, wrong-number cleanup, duration-threshold calibration |
| 2 | **Lead status (stage) + timestamp of change** | In-app or CRM sync | Funnel conversion by rep, stage velocity, outcome-validated attempt and duration thresholds; makes "going cold" precise |
| 3 | **Lead created_at + source** | Webhook or CSV from IndiaMART, 99acres, Meta forms, CRM | True speed-to-lead for portal leads, source ROI |
| 4 | **Rep roster: shift, role (desk/field), status** | Admin settings | Fair occupancy and idle metrics, orphaned-lead detection |
| 5 | **Deal value** (optional) | Lead field | Revenue per talk-hour |

For BFSI customers, a sixth field matters: a **"customer requested callback at this time" override**. Without it, a legitimate evening callback requested by the borrower trips the RBI calling-window flag.

## Conclusion

The large customer's request is less about new data than about **a new unit of analysis and a new delivery model**. The customer phone number, sequenced across every rep, is the unit that turns Callyzer's five fields into decisions. A ranked, statistically gated, three-item brief is the delivery model that gets those decisions read. Competitors chase transcript AI, which is costly, consent-heavy and a poor fit for SIM-based Indian SMBs, so the metadata-native layer appears largely uncontested. Building it also produces the dataset that lets Callyzer replace US folklore with India's own numbers. The sequence for the next two quarters is to fix data trust and lag first, ship the Tier 1 catalogue as a morning brief with the large customer as design partner, add the one-tap disposition to begin calibrating proxies against outcomes, and release missed-call callback speed and attempt curves as the first public benchmarks.

The biggest risk is credibility, not engineering. Callyzer's existing connect-rate and best-hour claims, any duration-equals-quality messaging, and any use of the debunked follow-up statistics could be contradicted by its own 242M-call data or by a competitor's analyst. Published definitions, account-weighted medians, confidence intervals and legal sign-off on DPDP and TRAI positions should therefore come before the press release.
