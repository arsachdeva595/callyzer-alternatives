# Blog series brief: "What your call logs already know"

**Owner:** Callyzer content · **Source:** `reports/Call log data sales insights.md` · **Tools to link:** Lead Leak Calculator, Call Connect Rate Benchmark

## Goal
Make Callyzer the place Indian sales and telecalling heads learn which numbers actually show lost leads. Every post points to the gated lead magnet, and to a free tool where one fits.

## Audience
Sales heads, telecalling managers and founders of 5–200-person calling teams in India: real estate, EdTech, loans and insurance, healthcare, auto, solar, B2B distribution. They already track calls and talk time, export to Excel, and still can't see where leads are lost.

## The big idea
Call count and connect rate show how busy the team was. The money is in the sequence of calls to each customer: missed calls nobody returned, leads dropped after one try, leads going cold, two reps calling the same person. Callyzer already has that data.

## Rules for writers
- **Quote studies exactly.** Use the myths table in the report. Never use "21× more likely to close" or "80% need 5 follow-ups".
- **No invented India numbers.** Until the benchmarks are computed, say "no India benchmark exists yet" and link the calculator.
- **Link primary sources** (HBR, Velocify, Truecaller/Kantar, RBI) and say when data is US-only.
- **Each post has:** one formula box ("how to calculate it in your team"), one 3–5 step fix list, a calculator or playbook CTA at about 40% depth and at the end, and an FAQ block of 3–4 questions.
- **Tone:** plain, practical, India-specific examples (₹, lakh, WhatsApp, 99acres/IndiaMART leads). No hype.
- **Check before publishing:** don't cannibalise existing callyzer.co posts (cold-calling statistics, best time to call, daily call report). Refresh or interlink those instead.

## Distribution
- **Each post:** a LinkedIn newsletter issue ("Sales Growth Hacks") and an Instagram carousel of its myths/fix list, then a WhatsApp broadcast to existing leads.
- **Posts 4–5:** pitch to sales-community newsletters and Indian startup media as the myth-busting angle.
